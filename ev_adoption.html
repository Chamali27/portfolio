// Project Overview

This project analyzes the global transition from Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) vehicles to Electric Vehicles (EVs). It evaluates environmental impact through emission reductions, economic benefits through cost savings, and future adoption trends using predictive modeling with Prophet and logistic growth models.

Team: WACI Abeysekara  ·  RL Hewanayaka  ·  MZM Hussein  ·  MN Mohamed

// Key Questions Explored

How much CO₂ can be reduced through global EV adoption?
How do EVs compare with ICE vehicles environmentally?
What are the future EV adoption scenarios up to 2030?
Are EVs economically beneficial in the long term?

// Methodology

01
Data Collection

Used vehicle, energy, and emission datasets sourced from multiple global organizations and open data repositories.

02
Data Preprocessing

Cleaned, merged, and standardized datasets in Python for consistent cross-country global analysis.

03
Model Development

Built emission and cost comparison models contrasting EV and ICE vehicle lifecycles across regions.

04
Forecasting

Applied Prophet and logistic growth models to predict EV adoption trends through 2030.

05
Scenario Analysis

Simulated optimistic, business-as-usual, and pessimistic adoption scenarios to quantify outcome ranges.

// Key Insights

🌍
EVs have reduced over 5M tons of CO₂ globally, with increasing yearly impact.
⚡
EV growth shifts energy demand from fossil fuels to electricity.
💰
EVs provide long-term cost savings despite higher upfront costs.
🔋
Cleaner energy grids significantly enhance EV environmental benefits.
📈
Transport emissions could drop over 30% by 2030 under high adoption.
🏛️
Adoption is driven by policy support, subsidies, and charging infrastructure.
