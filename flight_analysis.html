// Project Overview

This project analyzes U.S. flight arrival performance using the Bureau of Transportation Statistics (BTS) On-Time dataset. The analysis covers over 100 million flights from 2010 to 2025, focusing on delays, cancellations, diversions, and operational efficiency. SQL Server was used for large-scale data transformation, while Power BI enabled interactive dashboards for trend analysis and decision-making insights.

// Key Questions Explored

How have flight delays, cancellations, and diversions evolved over time?
What are the main root causes of flight delays?
Which airlines experience the highest operational disruptions?
How does airport-level performance vary across the U.S.?

// Methodology

01
Data Collection

Retrieved flight performance data from the U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics (TranStats), covering 2010–2025.

02
Data Cleaning & Storage

Processed raw datasets in SQL Server, handled missing values, standardized delay metrics, and ensured data consistency.

03
Data Aggregation

Created optimized SQL views at airline and airport levels to compute KPIs such as delays, cancellations, and diversion rates.

04
Dashboard Development

Built interactive Power BI dashboards with DAX measures for delay impact, averages, and root cause analysis.

// Key Insights

📉
Flight disruptions dropped sharply in 2020 due to COVID-19, followed by a strong recovery trend in subsequent years.
⏱️
Over 20 million delay hours were recorded, making delays the most significant operational challenge compared to cancellations.
✈️
Late-arriving aircraft and carrier-related issues were the leading causes of delays, highlighting schedule dependency problems.
🌦️
Seasonal peaks in delays occur during mid-year (July), driven by high passenger demand and weather disruptions.
🏢
Smaller regional airports showed significantly higher average delays compared to major hubs, indicating localized inefficiencies.
🔄
Diversions are used frequently as an operational strategy, with a notably high system-wide diversion rate across all years.
