// Project Overview

This research paper addresses early customer churn — customers who leave within the first three months of subscription — using a hybrid of supervised machine learning and KMeans customer segmentation. Four ML models were compared, with LightGBM emerging as the top performer. SHAP values were used for model explainability, and threshold optimization was applied to maximize business cost savings.

Team: WACI Abeysekara  ·  RL Hewanayaka  ·  MZM Hussein  ·  MN Mohamed

// Key Questions Explored

Which customers are likely to churn within their first 3 months?
What are the strongest predictors of early churn behavior?
How can threshold optimization reduce misclassification costs?
What behavioral segments exist among early churners?

// Methodology

01
Data Collection & Preprocessing

Used the public Telco Customer Churn dataset (7,043 records, 21 features). Applied median imputation, label encoding, one-hot encoding, and StandardScaler normalization.

02
Class Imbalance Handling (SMOTE)

Only 8.48% of customers were early churners. Applied SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) to balance the training dataset before model training.

03
Model Training & Comparison

Trained and evaluated 4 models — Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM — using precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC with 75/25 stratified split.

04
Threshold Optimization & SHAP Explainability

Optimized LightGBM decision threshold to 0.236 for maximum business cost savings. Used SHAP values to identify key churn drivers: tenure, monthly charges, and contract type.

05
Customer Segmentation (KMeans)

Applied KMeans clustering on early churners to identify 3 actionable behavioral segments, each with distinct retention strategies and revenue loss estimates.

// Key Insights

🏆
LightGBM achieved the best overall performance with ROC-AUC of 0.995 and F1-score of 0.97, outperforming all other models including XGBoost and Random Forest.
⚙️
Threshold optimization at 0.236 (vs default 0.5) saved $14,550 in misclassification costs by maximizing recall of early churners while minimizing false positives.
📊
SHAP analysis revealed tenure, monthly charges, and contract type as the top 3 churn drivers — shorter tenure and higher monthly bills strongly predicted early exit.
👥
KMeans clustering identified 3 distinct churn segments: Price-Sensitive, Low-Engagement, and Onboarding Failure — each requiring different targeted retention strategies.
💸
The Onboarding Failure segment showed the highest revenue loss risk — high-spending customers churning very early, often due to poor onboarding experiences.
✅
5-fold cross-validation confirmed model robustness with a mean ROC-AUC of 0.996 (std: 0.0013), demonstrating strong generalizability on unseen data.
