// Project Overview

This project analyzes residential Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) data in Manchester to evaluate energy efficiency, CO₂ emissions, and energy costs. SQL Server was used for data cleaning and transformation, while Power BI dashboards were built to explore patterns across property types, energy ratings, and costs.

Team: WACI Abeysekara  ·  RL Hewanayaka  ·  MZM Hussein  ·  MN Mohamed

// Key Questions Explored

Which property types have the highest energy costs and emissions?
How do EPC ratings vary across Manchester properties?
What factors influence energy efficiency (walls, roofs, fuel types)?
What improvements can reduce costs and carbon emissions?

// Methodology

01
Data Collection

Collected UK EPC data (2006–2025) filtered for Manchester properties from official government datasets.

02
Data Cleaning

Processed in SQL Server, removed duplicates, handled missing values, and created structured views for analysis.

03
Data Modelling

Built Power BI data model and created DAX measures for KPIs such as energy costs, CO₂ emissions, and efficiency ratings.

04
Dashboard Development

Designed interactive dashboards with slicers for property type, tenure, and energy rating to support exploration.

// Key Insights

🔥
Heating accounts for approximately 70% of total energy costs, making it the single biggest cost driver.
🏠
Mid-terrace properties and cavity walls significantly impact overall energy consumption patterns.
🌍
Flats generally show lower CO₂ emissions compared to detached and semi-detached houses.
⚡
Most properties fall under EPC ratings C and D, indicating strong potential for efficiency improvements.
☀️
Solar panels and heating system upgrades provide the most significant cost and emission reductions.
📊
Over 1M+ improvement recommendations highlight widespread opportunities for sustainability gains.
